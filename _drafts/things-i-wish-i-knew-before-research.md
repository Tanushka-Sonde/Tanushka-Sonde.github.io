Since January 2025 I've done robotics research at IITGN (teleoperation, then a VLA sim-to-real pipeline), two industry internships building RAG systems, a research reproduction in retinal OCT, and now computer vision research on sketch-to-scene generation. Here's what I'd tell myself at the start.

## 1. Most of the work is not the interesting part

PROMPT: In the VLA project, the dataset and IK plumbing took most of the time. What fraction of your time went to setup, data and debugging versus "the model"?

## 2. Reproduce before you extend

PROMPT: The retinal OCT project: reproducing the paper first is what exposed the dataset confounds. What did reproducing teach you that reading the paper didn't?

## 3. Write down every experiment, including the failures

PROMPT: Tie this to the 24-trial grid and the Deflated Sharpe: you can't account for what you've forgotten you tried.

## 4. "It didn't finish" is still a result

PROMPT: OpenVLA fine-tuning wasn't completed. How did you frame that honestly, and what did the unfinished project still give you?

## 5. Ask earlier than feels comfortable

PROMPT: A time asking your professor or a labmate saved you days.

## 6. Hardware punishes assumptions

PROMPT: The teleop safety stops, controller modes, and needing to press C three times. What did real hardware teach you about testing?

## 7. Mechanical engineering helped more than I expected

PROMPT: Kinematics, controls, thinking in systems. Where did that background give you an edge in ML and robotics work?
